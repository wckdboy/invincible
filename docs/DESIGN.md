# Invincible: design system

This is how the PWA looks, moves and behaves, and why. It is the reference for anyone, human or agent, who changes `apps/web`. The tokens live at the top of `apps/web/src/index.html`; the components live in `apps/web/src/ui/`.

## Principles

1. **Monochrome; colour means something.** Invincible is black and white ink with slight gradients. Ink (white on dark, black on light) belongs to the owner: the one primary action on a screen (of the decisions a page holds, only the first keeps the ink: `ui/controls.ts` `onePrimary`), focus, and what you approved. Green, amber and red are the only colours, and they only ever mean status, trust, verdict or risk. Nothing is coloured for decoration. ("Don't compete for attention you haven't earned.")
2. **Show the work.** An agent that acts on your behalf has to be legible: what it is doing now, what it decided and why, what it will do next. Spinners alone are not an answer.
3. **Approvals scale with stakes.** Reading is free, a phone joins with one click after you compare codes, a machine asks once more because it receives the vault, and anything irreversible makes you type its name.
4. **Safe by construction.** No HTML from strings, keys only in password fields, secrets stopped before they leave the page. The safety patterns below are not optional styling: they're the product.
5. **Instant, then smooth.** Frequent actions don't animate. Infrequent ones get short, interruptible motion on `transform` and `opacity` only. Nothing shifts layout once loaded.
6. **One app, every body.** A phone in one hand and a 1440 px desktop are both first-class. Touch targets are 44 px where the pointer is coarse.

## Brand

The look is the owner's of 26 November 2026: the I-beam as the mark and as your agent, in one ink; the apps monochrome (black, white and greys, bold type), with colour kept for the agents, each a field in colours of its own, and red alone for what went wrong. Gold, amber, cream, blue at work and green done went that day (the iPhone took the look first: `apps/apple/Invincible/UI/Brand.swift`).

### The mark: the I-beam

**The I-beam** is the app's mark and its icon (the owner's choice, 26 November 2026: "simpler, still sleek and cool, logo, minimal bold monochrome"; it replaced Field · Aurum, the gold field). It is one solid form in one ink, the steel section and the letter I: three rectangles in a 100-unit box (`scripts/mark.ts`, `MARK_RECTS` and `MARK_POLYGON`), white on near black (`#0a0a0b`) as the icon. Each pixel's share of it is worked out exactly, never sampled: deterministic, no browser. In the page it is the mark itself, on no tile.

### Wordmark

"INVINCIBLE" in the system sans: capitals at 650 weight, 0.2em tracking, 12–13 px, beside the mark at the head of the sidebar (`.wordmark-text`; the Apple app's iPad and Mac sidebar, `Wordmark`; Android's sidebar). No custom lettering; the spacing does the work.

### The primary and its agents

Your agent, the primary, is the I-beam in one ink, still, wherever it is drawn (`ui/orb.ts`' `orb()`: Home's header, its row in the sidebar, its seat, a speaker in a conversation), never a field and never gold: where it stands is said in words beside it. Each agent is a field in colours of its own: one you chose, or one of core's 24 `AGENT_TINTS` its handle picks (`agentColors`, `lib/field-orb.ts` `agentField`). Embers drift off a field at work. *Motion* says when each moves; the gold field and its beam (`PRIMARY_FIELD`, `PRIMARY_BEAM`) are drawn nowhere now, and go with the agents' fields when the Grille replaces them.

**An agent's mark is the Grille** (O2: shown five directions on 26 November 2026, the owner chose "2 · Grille"; core's
`agent-mark.ts`), replacing each agent's field app by app; the primary stays the I-beam in one ink, never a Grille. A
solid disc in the agent's own colour, cut into slats by two or three slots at its own angle: twelve forms (six angles,
two or three slots). Its colour is one of eight, Amber, Lime, Green, Teal, Sky, Blue, Violet and Magenta (OKLCH 72 to 334
degrees: none between pink-red and red-orange, which are failure's), each in a light-page form at least 3:1 against the
page and a dark-page one. The node gives each agent its colour and form once, as it joins, after the agents before it:
its handle's colour or the next free one, so no two share a colour until all eight are taken, and its handle's form or
the next one nobody wears, so colour and form both differ while they can and a ninth agent repeats a colour but never a
form (`assignMark`). A colour you pick snaps to the nearest of the eight (`snapColor`), its form kept. Its states:
idle, solid and still; working, its slats slide in their slots, each in its own phase, a cycle every 5.5 s; asking you,
its top slat in ink and out of line, beating every 4.2 s; queued, pale; paused, grey; done, its slots close and open
once as it arrives (1.4 s), then still; failed, its bottom slat in the shared red, knocked loose about its middle (0.6
s), then still. The shape tells the state, never colour alone, and the red is a slat at most. Rows are always still,
reduced motion stills everything, and nothing is drawn at rest. Its sizes are 64, 44, 28, 20 and 16 pt, a slot never
narrower than 1.35 pt. Every app draws it from core's numbers (`markGeometry`, `markColors`), held to them by the
vectors' `marks`. Every agent's colour changed once when the marks came: agents from before were given theirs in the
order they joined, and a colour chosen for one was snapped.

### Starlight

The one decorative flourish, and it is barely there: a faint radial glow (`--glow`, `--glow-hero`) at the top of the canvas and behind the emblem on the welcome, pairing and empty-chat screens. Primary buttons and the active navigation item carry a slight top-to-bottom gradient.

### Voice

Courteous, candid, brief.

- Say what happened and what's next: "Sealed. Every full node can use it. No model ever sees it."
- Be plain about limits: "This device can't add keys. It was paired without the vault:write scope."
- Errors are courteous and offer a way forward ("Try again", "Open the vault"), never a stack trace or blame.
- Small touches of warmth are allowed where they cost nothing ("Good evening."). No puns, no emoji.

### Type

- **Sans:** the system stack (`ui-sans-serif, system-ui, -apple-system, "Segoe UI Variable Text", Roboto…`). Native on every platform, zero bytes, no external fonts (the CSP forbids them anyway).
- **Mono:** `ui-monospace, "SF Mono", "Cascadia Code", "JetBrains Mono", Menlo, Consolas`. Used for ids, model refs, commands, code, hints like `…3456`.
- **Scale (px):** 11 · 12 · 13 · 14 · 15 · 16 · 18 · 20 · 24 · 30 · 40. Body is 14 on desktop and 15 on phones; inputs are 16 on phones so iOS never zooms.
- **Weights:** 400 body · 550 labels and buttons · 600 headings · 650 page titles and figures.
- **Numbers that change** (counts, RTT, load, countdowns, scores) use `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` (`.tnum`), so they don't jitter.
- Headings tighten (−0.01 to −0.022em); small caps-style group labels (11 px, uppercase, +0.06em) are used only in the palette and the smart selector's groups.

## Motion

The tokens are at the top of `apps/web/src/index.html`; the iPhone, iPad, Mac and Android apps take the same numbers (a spring by its response and damping, which SwiftUI and Compose both take).

| Token | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `--ease-out` | `cubic-bezier(.23, 1, .32, 1)` | Almost everything: things arrive fast and settle |
| `--ease-exit` | `cubic-bezier(.4, 0, 1, 1)` | Leaving: a toast dropping away, a dialog closing. It gathers speed as it goes, and is always shorter than the arrival |
| `--ease-spring` | `linear(…)`: a spring of response 0.4 s, damping 0.72, sampled over `--d-spring` | What arrives: a dialog opening, a toast rising, the banner dropping in (their fades take `--d-enter` or `--d-2`, as springs don't fade). 3.8 % past its mark at most, settled by its end. SwiftUI `.spring(response: 0.4, dampingFraction: 0.72)`; Compose `spring(dampingRatio = 0.72f, stiffness = 246.7f)` |
| `--ease-in-out` | `cubic-bezier(.77, 0, .175, 1)` | The banner's line of light, the one breath |
| `--ease-drawer` | `cubic-bezier(.32, .72, 0, 1)` | Bottom sheets (Vaul's curve) |
| `--d-1` | 120 ms | Hover, press, colour changes, the palette |
| `--d-2` | 180 ms | Switches; an alert leaving (a toast, the banner, a dialog closing) |
| `--d-3` | 240 ms | Page enter, the stack of toasts moving back, a count rolling, the segmented thumb |
| `--d-enter` | 420 ms | An alert coming into focus: a toast, the banner |
| `--d-spring` | 500 ms | The spring's length (a dialog opening; its fade takes `--d-2`) |
| `--d-sheet` | 380 ms | Bottom sheets on phones |
| `--d-breath` | 2400 ms | One slow breath of something new that needs you, once |
| `--d-sweep` | 2600 ms | One pass of the banner's line of light: as it comes, and again at each Retry. Never a loop |

Rules:

- **Frequency decides.** Navigation, typing, sending and messages arriving are instant; the palette appears in 120 ms. Only rarer moments (a sheet, a toast, the welcome and pairing screens) move.
- **Transform and opacity only.** No animated height, width or layout. The one exception is the segmented thumb, which animates `width` alongside `transform` so it can match uneven labels. A toast coming in also clears a slight blur (a filter on one small surface, for its moment of arrival).
- **Interruptible.** Sheets follow the finger 1:1 while dragged and settle from wherever they are. A quick flick (over 0.45 px/ms) or a drag past 30% of the height dismisses.
- **Press feedback:** buttons scale to 0.97 while pressed.
- **Your agent's I-beam never moves** (it says where it stands in words beside it). **Only the agent's orb you are looking at moves, and slowly** (your words, 26 November 2026: the glyph orbs looked "almost like nipples", and "i dont want them to be too visually distractin"; 25 November: "slow and calming movement"). Every orb is a field (`lib/field.ts`, `lib/field-orb.ts`, the app icon's lines of force): the primary's in gold with **its beam** through it, each agent's in its own colours. No ring round any of them: the primary's heads-up ring went on 26 November 2026 (your words: "remove the old mark around [the primary]. make the primary agent distinct in some other cool way"), and what marks it out since is its beam: a blade of light along its field's own axis, out of both its poles, as a pulsar's jets go, narrowing to a point and fading from its lit gold to its rim's copper (`lib/field-orb.ts` `PRIMARY_BEAM`, `beamLine`, `beamAt`). It reads at every size it is drawn at, 18 px included, where a ring or sparks didn't; its field turns about it, and it shines as it does. No agent has one. Where orbs are many (rows, cards, the board, a conversation's history) they are still. The primary's orb where you talk to it, and an agent's in its own thread's header while it works, move in their mood (`lib/orb-motion.ts`' moods, `FIELD_MOTION`): a turn takes half a minute or more, a breath five seconds, and a new mood arrives over about two. The row's words and colour say the state, so motion is never the only telling:
  - **At rest** (idle): still (your word, 26 November 2026: "nothing animating at rest"; until then it turned, a turn a minute).
  - **Working**: it turns about its own axis, a turn in half a minute, and a few embers (five, small and soft, a light falling away from its middle, never a hard dot) drift off its burning rim and fade. An agent's at work only turns.
  - **Waiting on you** (needs you): a slow breath of brightness.
  - **Queued**: dimmer, and still. **Paused**: dimmer still, and still.
  - **Done** (verified, or new to you): it settles to a stop.
  - **Failed**: its rim dims towards red-brown.
  - An agent in any mood but work comes to rest, in that mood's light (dimmer while queued or paused, its rim red-brown when its work failed).
- **How it is drawn:** the field, its look at each size, its motion and its embers are data and pure functions of time (`lib/field.ts`, `lib/field-orb.ts`), held for the Apple and Android apps by the parity vectors (their `field` section), which draw it as the PWA does, its ink on a light page included (`UI/FieldOrbView.swift`, Android's `ui/Field.kt`). A field is made once (each line's longitude and width from its seed: `fieldModel`) and turned each frame (`fieldAt`). Its middle stays dark (no line comes into the core, and the picture's middle holds a tenth of its brightest light or less), and its lines are of uneven widths at uneven longitudes, never a lattice (a lattice of dots on a sphere read as a disco ball when the icon was chosen). Invincible's has 26 to 46 lines by its size; an agent's 12 to 20, and its lean and longitudes are its handle's, always seen from the side. The PWA draws one on a canvas its own size (`ui/orb.ts`): each line one continuous stroke from point to point, coloured by its light as the icon shows light (ACES at the icon's exposure, `fieldTone`), the strokes of one colour and brightness gathered into one path, so a frame is a few dozen draws; on a dark page they are added as light ("lighter") over a close glow three times as wide and an eighth as strong (no fog), and on a light page they are ink (since 26 November 2026, when the agents read there as thin scribbles, "flat line art"): each colour as a saturated ink in its own hue (OKLab: its chroma a fifth more, as far as sRGB goes, and as light as it can be while it stands 3.4:1 from the page for its key, 3.1:1 for its rim, never darkened towards black, which made gold brown), its lit lobe going towards a lighter sheen of its lit colour where the key strikes hardest (the light page's white-hot), as opaque as it is lit from a floor of a fifth (its far lines faint but there), one width at every size and none under half a pixel (`FIELD_INK`, `fieldInks`, worked out once for each field's colours, `fieldInk`, `FIELD_STROKE`). The primary's beam on a light page is a blade of that ink from its sheen through its gold to its copper, at least 1.7 px across at its root (it held only as a needle at 34 px), fading slower than its light does, over a close tint (`PRIMARY_BEAM.ink`). A still orb is drawn once, and the same one again (an agent in twenty rows) is stamped from that picture; a change of theme draws every orb on the page again. A moving one is drawn on the page's one clock (`ui/motion.ts`): only on screen, nothing while the page is hidden, and only as often as its motion needs (`fieldFps`: 30 frames a second while a mood arrives, 20 while its embers drift or it breathes, 15 while it only turns, none once it has come to rest, which is wherever nothing is going on). In headless Chrome on the owner's MacBook, making and drawing one takes the page's thread 0.12 ms for an agent's 34 px face, 0.22 ms for its 50 px orb and 0.6 ms for its 160 px welcome (with the processor slowed 4× at three pixels to one: 0.44, 0.88 and 2.7 ms); a frame of one already made, about as much. Measured against the app just before it (512d6b7e), one after the other in Chrome 156 on that MacBook (`apps/web/measure/first-load.ts --runs 5 --shifts`): at rest, the agents' main thread works 0.7 ms a second where it worked 80.1 (the whole browser 137.5 ms of processor a second, where 336.9), and Chat's 8.6 where 37.5 (63.2, where 175.5); on the phone profile every page is usable sooner, each range apart from before (the agents 785 to 704 ms on a first visit, Chat 765 to 722, a room 835 to 742), with fewer elements (Chat 450 to 364); `/app.js` is 1.5 KB smaller as brotli (205.4 to 203.9 KB); and nothing on any place moves once it is on screen, before or after. (Drawing orbs in a worker of their own, off the page's thread, was tried: the page's thread was then nearly idle, but the browser as a whole worked a third more in Chrome and nearly twice as much in WebKit.) An answer in a conversation's history, and a speaker beside something it said earlier, stay still; the answer it is writing moves.
- **A dot** for a state is a small orb in its colour, lit from above; one for something going on is lit, a soft ring of its light round it, and still. **Nothing animates at rest** (your word, 26 November 2026: a hyper-modern interface with nothing animating at rest): what repeats for ever says only that something loads or is being written (a spinner, a skeleton's shimmer, the writing caret, the boot screen's breath), and a test holds the stylesheet to that list.
- **Something new that needs you breathes once** (`arrive()` in `ui/controls.ts`): a dot's light swells from it and fades, a count glows round and settles, over `--d-breath`, once. Never a loop that keeps pulling the eye; nothing where motion is reduced.
- **A count that changes rolls** to its new figure (`setCount()`, `lib/alerts.ts` `countRoll`): up as it grows, down as it shrinks, over `--d-3`; drawn the first time, or showing the same figure (99+ to 99+), it doesn't move.
- **Reduced motion:** when the system asks, or Settings says *Reduced*, every duration becomes ~0 and shimmer, caret blinking and the boot screen's breath stop: what repeats is taken away rather than quickened, as a loop made near instant would flicker (a test holds every repeating animation in the stylesheet to this; a spinner alone turns on, as it is what says work is under way). Settings can also force *Full*. JS-driven motion checks `prefersReducedMotion()`; the orbs' clock (`ui/motion.ts`) checks it every frame, so a change in Settings or on the device stills them, or sets them going, at once.

Opening the app while the node can't be reached shows a calm full screen that tries again on its own (3 s, then doubling to every 30 s; at once when the network returns or the app comes to the front), so a node restart heals without a tap. The first request waits at most 12 s. When a newer app has been fetched in the background, a toast offers **Reload**.

## Smart selector

Where a view had a cluster of controls (role buttons, toggles, rows of filter chips), it now has **one control that says what's chosen in plain words** and opens the choices. Rarely changed options live inside it; nothing used often gets harder to reach (Chats and New chat stay buttons beside it).

- **The control** (`.selector-trigger`): an optional icon or status dot, the current choice in words (ellipsised, the full text in its name and tooltip), a chevron. A non-default choice, such as a filter that's on, is shown in ink (`data-active`). `icon-only` makes a ⋯ menu of it.
- **The choices**: a popover anchored under the control (above it when there's more room there); a bottom sheet on narrow screens (under 640 px) with thumb-sized rows (48 px) where the pointer is coarse, where the keyboard waits until you tap the search field. Groups have small-caps labels and choose **one** (a check), toggle **many** (a box) or run an **action**. Options carry an icon, a meta line (a model, a count, what it means), a status dot, or the reason they can't be chosen.
- **Finding**: a search field past seven options (fuzzy, by label, meta and keywords; matches marked in ink; each new query makes its best match the one Enter chooses); without one, typing jumps to the option that starts with it. The likely choices come first: the caller orders them (projects by last use).
- **Keys**: ArrowDown on the control opens it; arrows move and skip what can't be chosen; Enter (or Space in the list) chooses; Esc closes and focus goes back to the control. Choosing one closes at once; toggles stay open.
- **Using it**: `selector({ label, current: () => "…" | { text, icon, tone, active }, groups: () => [...], onSelect: (group, option) => { change state; } , scope })` returns `{ el, refresh, open, close }`. `groups()` and `current()` are read every time they're shown, so the control always tells the truth; call `refresh()` after a change from elsewhere.
- **Where**: the chat's one ⋯ menu, the Activity filter, the Archive's kind, time and trust.

## Agentic patterns

- **Process view, not a spinner.** Each answer shows its steps as they happen: *Asked as planner* → *Read 3 archived entries* (which ones, by id and title, untrusted ones marked; only when the archive had something relevant) → *Thinking with anthropic/claude-opus-5-5* → (*… didn't answer: reason*, on fallback) → each tool Invincible uses, with its icon, its title and arguments, and how it went (*Bank balances* `{"account":"…"}` → *data*; a refused or failed call is amber) → *Answering*. When it's done, the steps fold into one line (`planner · model · 4.2 s · 2 tools · 1 fallback`) that you can open again.
- **Stop is always one click away:** while it works the composer shows Stop, Esc stops, and from any other page the top bar shows "Answering… Stop". Stop is said to the node, and it is the only thing that stops it: the turn runs there, so it goes on while you look elsewhere, lock the phone or reload, and the page that comes back (or another device) shows it from its start. Enter never stops an answer.
- **An agent is one timeline** (`views/team.ts`, `lib/timeline.ts`): its own conversation with you and every task given to it, wherever it was given, in the order they happened; a room's timeline holds the room's words and tasks. A task is a **card** where it was given, and it stays there while it runs and after (`views/task-card.ts`, its words in `lib/task-card.ts`): its title and a badge (Queued; Running, in ink; Verified, green; Not verified, amber; Failed, red; Stopped; Interrupted, amber), then who gave it to the agents when it wasn't given by name, the room it was given in when that was another, and what it waits for.
  - **While it runs**, its latest six lines in mono, the newest in full ink. They are asked of the node only for what is new (`POST /api/team/trail` with `after`), every 1.5 s while the card is on screen and the app is in front, and never once the task has ended. They are a log that is not read aloud as it grows.
  - **Once it has ended**: why it wasn't verified, a run's own ending in words ("It gave up.") in a callout (red for a failure, amber for an interruption, quiet for a stop of yours); what the agent said of it (its own summary, four lines at most); what its run came to (`taskRecordFacts`, the terminal's words); the files its changes kept and the artifact that holds them (it opens in Artifacts); and, folded, *What it did* (everything the node kept of its lines, read when it is opened) and *The task* (goal, commands, result).
  - Work in hand can be **steered** (*Steer…*: a sheet for your words, taken at its next safe point) or **stopped**; work that ended unverified can be **resumed**, or **given again** as an editable copy of what was asked, with what came of the last try under it; verified work that kept its changes can be **shipped** as a pull request. Items that haven't changed are handed back as they are (`ui/dom.ts`' `syncChildren`), so a running trail and an opened disclosure survive the 2 s refresh.

The answer replaces the form: "Rio takes it", the node's reasons in a line, then how each of the others stood, those that couldn't saying why; **Open Rio** goes to its timeline, where the card says it was given to the agents. A task none can take is refused in the node's words, in the sheet.

Nothing on the page buys, rents or installs anything, and it says so. On Evals, **By effort** shows the Index per effort level and a verdict callout: green only when more compute measurably helped.
- **Agents** (on Fleet) lists the coding agents that hold their own key to Invincible (MCP arms) with what each may do; **Revoke** cuts one off at once (type its name to confirm), from any device. Giving an agent a key, like raising a device's permissions, stays at a terminal on a node.
- **Permissions in the UI, not in settings:** Settings shows this device's scopes as granted or not; invites let you choose what a new phone may do (Everything / Chat & read / Custom); pages explain why an action is missing instead of failing.
- **Undo where possible:** clearing a conversation offers Undo. Irreversible actions confirm instead.
- **Placement playground:** "Where would this run?" ranks machines for a described piece of work, with reasons and rejections, and runs nothing.

## Safety patterns

These are enforced in code and, where possible, in tests (`packages/node/test/assets.test.ts`, `apps/web/src/lib/lib.test.ts`).

1. **No HTML from strings.** Everything is built with `createElement` / `createElementNS` / `textContent`. There is no helper that accepts HTML, and the build test fails if `innerHTML`, `outerHTML`, `insertAdjacentHTML`, `document.write` or `createContextualFragment` appear in a bundle. Model output goes through a parser that emits an AST; raw HTML stays literal text.
2. **An agent's screen** is shown only as a JPEG or PNG in base64 from the node, as a `data:` image (`img-src 'self' data:`), never as anything else it might send. What you type on it goes to its VM when you send it and nowhere else: it is not kept in the page or as a draft, the field is emptied once it is sent, and the ledger records only how many characters.
3. **Links from models** are only ever `http(s)`, open in a new tab with `noopener noreferrer nofollow`, and carry the full URL as a tooltip. The real host is shown next to the link unless its text is itself a URL on that same host, so look-alike text can't disguise where it goes. Link text never contains another link. Images are never fetched (`img-src 'self' data:`).
4. **Keys** are typed or pasted only into `secretInput()`: `type=password`, `autocomplete=off`, `spellcheck=false`, no autocapitalise, password-manager opt-outs, and the browser's own reveal button hidden. The value is read once on submit and the field is cleared immediately, including on errors and when the sheet closes. It is never stored, logged or shown again; only the server's hint (`…3456`) is displayed.
5. **Secrets stopped at the door.** Chat and memory run `findSecrets` while you type and again at the moment of sending. A finding blocks sending and offers **Store it in the vault instead** (moves the detected key into the vault form and clears the message) or **Remove it from my message**. A chat draft is kept in memory between pages only when it has nothing secret-shaped in it. The server's 422 is shown as the same calm warning.
6. **Pairing:** a key store that can't be read just now (Safari drops its storage connection after a Home Screen app has slept) is tried again and then shown as exactly that, with Reload; never as "not paired", which would invite pairing a new key over the enrolled one. The SAS is shown large; the invite proof never leaves the page; requests go to `location.origin` only; the server's SAS and the approval proof are verified before the device trusts anything. Pairing survives a reload (phones discard tabs while you walk to the laptop): on load the page asks, with its status proof, whether it already asked or was approved, and network hiccups retry. The `#pair=` hash is cleared as soon as pairing ends, whether it succeeded or not. The pairing code (with the invite schema) is its own bundle, `/pair.js`, loaded only when an invite link is opened.
7. **Typed confirmation** for the most dangerous actions: revoking a device and deleting a secret. A plain confirmation for rotating the vault, destroying a sandbox, forgetting a memory, removing a provider and forgetting this browser. Approving a *machine* asks once more, because it receives the vault.
8. **Scope-aware:** destinations and actions follow `whoami.scopes`. Missing permissions are explained, not hidden behind errors.
   - A refused request isn't taken as proof of revocation. The app asks once more, and if the node's clock disagrees with this device's by more than a minute it says so ("This device's clock is off") instead of suggesting you forget a perfectly good key. When it does leave the app, polling and shortcuts stop with it.
9. **Storage:** `localStorage` holds UI preferences only (`invincible:theme`, `invincible:motion`, `invincible:sidebar`, `invincible:chat-role`, `invincible:chat-enter`: what Enter does while it works, `invincible:open-chat`: the id of the kept chat you had open, never a word of it, so a reload comes back to it, and how the agents is laid out here: `invincible:agents-view` (board or list), `invincible:roster-folded` (the sidebar's folded sections), `invincible:agent-panel` (an agent's side panel hidden)). Each is read and written in a try/catch, and the page works the same without them. The page you were last on isn't kept: the app opens on Invincible, and an address survives a reload by itself. The device key is a non-extractable WebCrypto key in IndexedDB. Chats aren't kept on the device, nor is what you queued behind them: the node keeps them (privately, deletable), and a temporary chat lives only in the tab. `sessionStorage` isn't used at all (a test fails the build if it is). Settings lists all of this under *Kept on this device*.
10. **CSP-clean:** no inline scripts or handlers, no external origins; inline styles only (allowed by `style-src 'unsafe-inline'`), and only with constant or numeric values.

## Accessibility

- AA contrast throughout (see Tokens); visible 2 px focus rings (`--focus`) on everything focusable.
- Full keyboard use: palette, shortcuts, arrow keys in segmented controls, the palette and smart selectors (ArrowDown on a selector opens it; typing filters or jumps), ↑ ↓ Home End through Home's rows and the drawer's (`ui/dom.ts` `arrowsThrough`), Enter/Esc in dialogs, Up/Down history in the sandbox console. A task's thread shows a ring inside its edge when Tab reaches it, so the keys scroll it.
- Dialogs use `showModal()`, label themselves and return focus to where you were.
- Chat has one persistent live region: it says when Invincible starts answering and reads the answer once it's complete (never token by token). Lists that refresh in the background (the ledger, fleet health) are not live regions, and background refreshes keep keyboard focus and opened sections where they were. Toasts announce through polite or assertive live regions, are reached with ⌥T (Alt+T) and put away with Esc, and an alert's controls (a toast's, the banner's Retry, a sheet's close) take 44 px to touch however small they are drawn. Counts, meters and the SAS code have text alternatives.
- Icons are decorative (`aria-hidden`) unless they carry meaning alone, in which case they get a label. Segmented controls keep an accessible name even when a phone hides their text.
- 44 px targets on touch screens: what is drawn smaller (a small button, Home's filters, the machine chip, a fold's failures) keeps its look and reaches past its edge to 44. Inputs at 16 px on phones.
- At 200 % text on a phone (a 195 px wide page) nothing runs off the screen: the top bar's title gives way to its buttons, a button's words end in an ellipsis, a badge's and a row's controls wrap, a choice drops its icon, the machine chip is its dot, and a message box's hint stays one line.

## Bundles

| File | What | Loaded |
|---|---|---|
| `/boot.js` | Theme and motion before first paint (~0.3 KB) | Always, blocking, in `<head>` |
| `/app.js` | The app (~480 KB; ~145 KB as brotli). Script is a small part of a phone's work (style and layout weigh more: measured below), so views aren't split out of it | Always; cached by the service worker |
| `/pair.js` | The pairing flow with the invite schema (zod) | Only when a `#pair=` link is opened |
| `/sw.js` | Offline shell, stale-while-revalidate | Registered on first visit |

The page carries all the CSS, and is sent without its stylesheet's comments (`servedPage` in `packages/node/src/assets.ts`; they stay in the source). Every text file (the page, the scripts, the manifest, the SVG icons) is sent with brotli (quality 9) or gzip when the browser accepts it, compressed on its first request and kept, and carries an `ETag`. Everything is `no-cache` ("ask first"), so a browser or the service worker that already holds this version gets a `304` and no body: opening the installed app costs a revalidation, not the whole app again.

The PWA imports only what it needs from `@invincible/core` through subpath exports (`/bytes`, `/identity`, `/pairing`, `/request-auth`, `/secret-scan`), which keeps zod out of daily use.

### What opening the app costs

`bun apps/web/measure/first-load.ts` measures it, and measures any checkout the same way (`--repo <checkout>`), so a change to the app is shown with its number before and after; `--shots <dir>` also takes screenshots at 390 and 1280 px, light and dark, at the same addresses on any checkout: every place the app lists, each as far down as it goes, the sheets and forms they open, and the components' gallery (`--runs 0` takes only those; `--places a,b` only some); `--shifts` measures what moves on each place (see *Nothing shifts*); `--rest` only what Chat and the agents cost at rest, the page's main thread and the processor time of its whole renderer and of the whole browser. It starts a throwaway node on loopback (a temp home, stand-in models, a stand-in Docker), gives it seven agents (one with a desktop in its VM), a room, tasks (running, waiting, verified with kept changes, given up) and two eval runs, a kept chat, a run in hand and a key it asked for, pairs a headless Chrome through the real pairing page, and opens Chat, the agents, a room, the Overview and a kept chat **cold** (nothing cached, no service worker: the first visit after pairing) and **warm** (the service worker holds the shell: every visit after that). It does so on the machine as it is ("laptop", 1280×800) and with the processor slowed 4× on a line of 9 Mbit/s down and 60 ms a request ("phone", 390×844: an emulation, not a phone). *Usable* is the first frame after the page's own content has replaced every loading placeholder (for a kept chat, once its answer is drawn).

A first visit to Chat on the laptop is 28 requests and `/sw.js`, 13 of them to the API. The app's nine files come to 629 KB (197 KB as brotli), and three quarters of that is `/app.js`.

What the numbers say:

- **The pages that changed open faster on a phone, every round.** The agents (what a phone opened on until 23 November 2026, `#/team`) was 656, 641 and 642 ms before and 585, 533 and 506 after, cold, and about 140 ms faster warm; a room 692, 671 and 680 before and 642, 638 and 614 after. In every round their ranges were apart. The page that listed them is now the roster, with fewer elements.
- **The pages that didn't change are where they were.** Their ranges overlapped in all three rounds but one: the Overview on the phone profile, cold, 633 ms before and 669 after in the second round (659 and 670 in the first, 637 and 637 in the third). What they carry is the new code: `/app.js` is 20.4 KB larger (6.4 KB as brotli), the page 4.2 KB (0.7 KB), about 6 ms more to fetch on the phone's line; the main thread's work on them did not grow. Chat on the phone profile was 590, 576 and 580 ms before and 609, 599 and 574 after: it now opens Invincible's most recent conversation where it opened an empty new chat, which is one more request on a device that has none remembered; the same kept chat at its own address was 572 and 587 ms before and 581 and 580 after.
- **What each part costs, in `/app.js` as brotli:** the roster as home 2.2 KB, an agent's timeline with its task cards 1.4 KB, giving a task to the agents 1.2 KB, the desktop choice in the forms 0.2 KB, the desktop viewer 1.5 KB.
- **A first visit waits on round trips, not on the processor.** On the phone profile the main thread works about 103 ms of Chat's 574: layout 40, paint 17, script 17, style 14, parsing 9. The rest is four waits one after another: the page, `/app.js`, `whoami`, then the page's own data.
- **Script is the small part.** Running `/app.js` takes under 2 ms on the laptop and about 6 ms slowed 4× (Chrome's own trace). Splitting the views out (about three fifths of the file) would shorten only a first visit's download and add a round trip the first time each page is opened; on every later visit `/app.js` comes from the service worker's cache. So it stays one file.
- **A page at rest should ask nothing of the processor.** The agents costs the main thread about 2.6 to 3 ms a second at rest on the laptop (2.2 to 2.3 before): the team every two seconds, and on a wide screen the sidebar's roster on every page (the team every six seconds, Invincible's work every fifteen). Since 25 November 2026 none of that is asked on a timer: the node's live stream says when the team, the turns or the runs changed, and only that is read again (the timers come back, at those paces, only while the stream is down). A chat costs about 1.8 (1.3 to 1.6 before). On a wide screen every page but Chat makes two or three more requests as it opens, for the roster beside it.

Timings move with the machine's load: measure two checkouts one after the other, and trust a difference only when their ranges don't overlap. Bytes, requests and element counts don't move.
